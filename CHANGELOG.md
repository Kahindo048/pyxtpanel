## [0.0.1] - 2026-11-18

First release.

### Added
- Repository, tooling configuration and package skeleton (architecture validated 2026-11-07).
- Panel declaration (`xtset`), calendar time axis (integers with `delta`, dates, Stata `%t` formats), selection, gaps, `tsfill`, balance classes, `.dta` read/write with `xtset` characteristics.
- Time-series operators `L F D S` and Stata notation (`"L2D.x"`, `"L(1/3).x"`) on the calendar: `PanelData.operate`, `lag`, `lead`, `diff`, `seasonal`; validated against Stata on 12 panels.
- Transformations: `within` (unit, period, two-way with a convergence report), `between`, `quasi_demean` and `theta_re`, forward orthogonal deviations `fod`, group means and counts, `rolling`, `cumulative`, `growth`; generic `PanelData.transform(name, ...)` over a registry of operators. Validated against `xtdata`, `regress ... i.id i.t` and `xtreg, re`.
- Array contract for the estimation packages: `PanelData.arrays(columns, where=...)` returns a frozen `PanelArrays` (`pyxtpanel.contract`, `CONTRACT_VERSION = 1`) with `offsets`, `time_index`, `lag_index`, `reindex_back`, `as_cube`.
- `PanelBase.with_frame` to reuse the sorted index when only the values change.
- Description of panels: `PanelData.xtdescribe` (participation patterns, distribution of T_i), `xtsum` (overall, between, within; `by=`), `xttab`, `xttrans` (gaps bridged as in Stata or `gaps="strict"`, `bins=`), `xtcorr` (overall, within, between with pair counts), `autocorr`, `attrition` (entries, exits, Kaplan-Meier survival of units), `missing_report` and `complete_cases`. Validated against Stata 17 (`xtdescribe`, `xtsum`, `xttab`, `xttrans`, `xtdata` + `correlate`).
- Public API: `as_panel`, `PanelLike`, top-level functions for every description and `operate`/`transform`; `Result.to_markdown` and `to_latex` for all results; HTML display of panels; table of kept Stata return codes (`STATA_CODES`).
- Tooling: `tools/stata_map.py` (generates `docs/stata_map.md`), `tools/snapshot_api.py` (API snapshot test), `tools/nouveau.py` (template for a new operator or description), `tools/avancement.py` (plan progress).
- Figures (extra `plot`, needs matplotlib): `PanelData.plot(kind, ...)` with `lines` (like `xtline`), `scatter` (between and within), `distribution`, `participation` and `transition`; same chart style as `pyxtdpthresh`, English and French (decimal comma), PNG/PDF/SVG export, `python -m tools.galerie`.
- Exploration tools (block K, no Stata equivalent): `PanelData.gap_types` (late entry, early exit, internal gap, re-entry), `unbalancedness` (Ahrens-Pincus gamma and nu as `plm::punbalancedness`), `balance_cost` (units and observations kept by each minimum T_i and each window, largest balanced block), `usable_equations` (equations left for a dynamic-panel estimator with first differences or orthogonal deviations; same counts as `pyxtabond2`). Registry `TOOLS` with the validation method of each tool.
- Exploration tools, continued (block K): `variation`, `residual_variation` (what within, first differences and FOD leave), `spells`, `event_time` (indicator or event period), `within_outliers` (modified z-score, flagged not removed) and `level_shifts`. Compared with Stata (`egen`, `bysort`, calendar lags) on 8 simulated panels; `egen median()` is imprecise in float, noted in `docs/stata_map.md`.
- Conversions and duplicates (block K): `PanelData.to_wide` (as `reshape wide`, checked against Stata), `to_long` (as `reshape long`, on a DataFrame), `PanelData.to_array3d` (variable x period x unit with the mask of rows) and `resolve_duplicates` (first, last, mean, sum, min, max, median, with a report of what was merged).
- Panel audit (block K): `xt.audit(panel_or_frame)` and `PanelData.audit()` run the registered checks (structure, balance, missing values, variation, outliers, equations with `dependent=`) and return an `AuditReport` of alerts with advice in French and English, exportable to text, Markdown, HTML (Jupyter), LaTeX. Thresholds are adjustable; raw data with repeated pairs get a critical alert. `python -m tools.nouveau controle <name>` creates a new check. State of the art completed (ETAT_DES_LIEUX section 7): `xtbalfront`, `tsspell` and `tsreport` are the Stata neighbours of `balance_cost`, `spells` and `gap_types`.
- Documentation: one reference document, `docs/documentation.md` (parts 0 to 9: getting started, concepts, declaring, operators, description, exploration tools, figures, the guide to the statistical tests, authors of estimation packages, method and validation) with five generated annexes (functions with their Stata and R equivalents, one card per test, return codes and errors, Stata behaviours reproduced, validation journal): `python -m tools.doc --write`. A LaTeX and PDF version is built from the same Markdown (`python -m tools.doc --latex` or `--pdf`, needs pandoc and xelatex; `docs/latex/header.tex`). Every Python block runs in `tests/contract/test_documentation.py`; the annexes must be current and every registered function, operator, figure, tool and test must be named in its part; the table of part 7 on balance and holes is re-run against the tests.
- Performance (block I): `xtset` sorts once (1 million shuffled rows: 0.44 s to 0.27 s), results are put back on the rows of the frame by a scatter instead of a `reindex` (`lag` 0.16 s to 0.10 s), `xtcorr` shares the common-row mask between its three kinds (0.72 s to 0.48 s); `xt.xtset` no longer loads `scipy.stats` (about one second at first use: the statistical tests are reached through `api/_lazy.py`). Invariance tests (shuffled rows, renamed identifiers, shifted or rescaled time) need no Stata. Fixed: `xtcorr` returned rounding noise as the correlation of a variable that is constant; it is now missing. - Figures, continued: `plot("states")` (units by periods coloured by state, like `panelview`, missing cells distinct), `plot("attrition")` (units observed, entries and exits, Kaplan-Meier survival) and `plot("balance_cost")` (share kept by minimum length and by window, largest block marked); gallery now has 10 figures.
- Statistical tests, infrastructure (block L.1): `TestResult` (statistic, p-value, degrees of freedom, hypotheses in French and English, `to_frame`, `summary`, HTML), registry `TESTS` with `TestSpec` (`register_test`, `list_tests`, `run_test`), normalised inputs (`panel_variables` refuses an unbalanced panel or a gap and names the first offending unit; `as_array` for category B). A contract test requires, for every registered test, a validation case run in Stata 17 and frozen in `tests/golden/` (no exception). `python -m tools.nouveau test NAME --famille F --categorie A|B` creates the module, its unit test and its Stata case. Families added for residual and specification tests (plan L.8): `heteroskedasticity`, `effects`, `normality`, `specification`, `heterogeneity`.
- `hansen` and `sargan` (category B): J and Sargan tests of over-identifying restrictions from bare arrays (moments, weight matrix, number of parameters, scale); `moment_covariance`; remarks for a model that is not over-identified or has more instruments than groups. Validated against Stata 17 `ivregress` + `estat overid` and `xtabond2` (relative difference below 2e-10). `TestResult.notes` holds translatable `Note` objects.
- `hausman` (consistent versus efficient estimator, Stata's generalised inverse and degrees of freedom, negative statistic reported as Stata does) and `wald` (joint chi-square or F test of `R b = r`), both from bare arrays. Validated against Stata 17 `hausman` and `test` on coefficients and covariances exported from `xtreg`, `regress` and `ivregress`.
- `ar_test` (category B): Arellano-Bond m_k test of serial correlation, robust form, from `Equations` (residuals, regressors, instruments, calendar positions) with the weight matrix and covariance of the estimator; `min_pairs` for estimators that require a minimum. Validated against Stata 17 `xtabond2` (`e(ar1)`, `e(ar2)`, one-step and two-step, balanced, gaps, entry and exit) and `xtabond` + `estat abond`. `wald_groupwise` (`xttest3`), `lm_random_effects` (`xttest0`), `f_effects` (F of `xtreg, fe`), `skewness_kurtosis` (`sktest`, with Royston's adjustment) and `jarque_bera`, all from bare arrays and validated against Stata 17. `breusch_pagan` (variants `normal`, `iid`, `fstat`) and `white` heteroskedasticity tests from residuals and regressors, validated against `estat hettest` and `estat imtest, white`; auxiliary least squares in `diagnostics/_shared/ols.py`. `diff_hansen` (C statistic of the exogeneity of an instrument subset, from the two Hansen statistics; validated against the difference-in-Hansen tests of `xtabond2`). The category B tests are importable from `pyxtpanel.diagnostics` (`hansen`, `sargan`, `hausman`, `wald`, `ar_test`, `moment_covariance`).
- First category A tests (they read a declared panel and variable names): `pesaran_cd` (Pesaran CD test of cross-sectional dependence on a variable or on residuals added to the frame; each pair of units uses its common periods with weight `sqrt(T_ij)`, so it accepts unbalanced panels and refuses, naming the two units, a pair with fewer than 3 common periods) and `wooldridge` (Wooldridge test of first-order autocorrelation from first differences, with a covariance clustered by unit; differences and lags follow the calendar, so holes, entry and exit are accepted as in Stata). Validated against Stata 17: `xtcsd, pesaran` after `xtreg, fe` (balanced, entry/exit/holes, holes), `xtcd2, noest` (balanced) and `xtserial` (AR(1) errors, independent errors, holes, entry and exit). Finding: on an unbalanced panel `xtcd2` does not use the formula of `xtcsd` (it fills the panel and correlates unit means with all non-missing values), so its CD differs; this package follows Pesaran (2015) and `xtcsd`.
- `harris_tzavalis` (category A, unit roots): Harris-Tzavalis test of a common AR parameter for large N and fixed T, with `deterministics` (`none`, `constant`, `trend`), `demean` and `small_sample_t`; needs a strongly balanced panel without gaps. Validated against Stata 17 `xtunitroot ht` (6 cases, relative difference below 1e-9).
- `fisher` (category A, unit roots): Fisher-type test from the p-values of one augmented Dickey-Fuller test per unit (`method`: inverse chi-squared `P`, inverse normal `Z`, logit `L`, modified `Pm`), fixed `lags`, `deterministics` (`constant`, `trend`, `drift`), `demean`; unbalanced panels and holes accepted; per-unit table in `details`. Validated against Stata 17 `xtunitroot fisher, dfuller` (6 cases, relative difference below 1e-9). Embedded numerical tables (`diagnostics/_shared/tables.py`): CSV files with a manifest (source, date, verification, SHA-256) that is checked on every load; first table: MacKinnon (1994) p-values of the Dickey-Fuller statistic, checked against statsmodels and Stata.
- `levin_lin_chu` (category A, unit roots): Levin-Lin-Chu test with the bias-adjusted `t*` (models `none`, `constant`, `trend`; fixed lags or lags chosen by AIC, BIC or HQIC; Bartlett, Parzen and quadratic-spectral kernels; fixed, `llc` or `nwest` bandwidth; `demean`; `interpolate`); needs a strongly balanced panel without gaps. Adjustment table `llc_adjustments.csv` (Levin, Lin and Chu 2002, Table 2). Validated against Stata 17 `xtunitroot llc` (12 cases, relative difference below 1e-9). `python -m tools.manifest_tables --write|--check` maintains the checksums of the embedded tables.
- `im_pesaran_shin` (category A, unit roots): Im-Pesaran-Shin test for heterogeneous panels. Without `lags`: `t-bar` (with the exact critical values of the paper for a balanced panel), `t-tilde-bar` and `Z-t-tilde-bar` (the returned statistic, no p-value when a unit is too short); with `lags` (fixed, or the maximum with `lag_selection` aic/bic/hqic): `W-t-bar`. `deterministics` constant or trend, `demean`, `interpolate`; unbalanced panels without gaps accepted. Tables `ips_table1/2/3.csv`. Validated against Stata 17 `xtunitroot ips` (12 cases, relative difference below 1e-9). Note: Stata's Table 3 has -2.204 for (trend, 8 lags, T = 60), apparently a transposition of -2.024; kept to equal Stata and flagged in the manifest.
- Calling the tests on a panel: `unit_root`, `cross_section_dependence` and `serial_correlation` (top-level functions and `PanelData` methods), the generic `PanelData.test(name, ...)`, Stata subcommand names as aliases (`ht`, `llc`, `ips`, `cd`, `xtserial`; `run_test` accepts them too). No default test; an option the test does not have, a missing argument, a test of another family, a test on arrays or an undeclared frame is refused before running, in French or English, with the arguments the test accepts. A contract test checks that the balance each test declares is the balance it enforces.
- Cross-section dependence, continued: `lm_cross_section` (Breusch-Pagan LM, alias `xttest2`; validated against an independent Mata implementation because `xttest2` is not installed), `friedman` and `frees` (Spearman correlations over the periods common to all units, as `xtcsd` does; table `frees_q.csv` of Frees' Q critical values; validated against `xtcsd, friedman` and `xtcsd, frees`), and the descriptive `cross_correlation` (matrix of correlations between units over each pair's common periods, with the average correlation and absolute correlation; pairs too short are `NaN`). Shared brick `diagnostics/_shared/pairwise.py`. `pesaran_cd` now uses it; its results are unchanged.
- Size and power by simulation for the 17 panel tests of category A that exist (`breitung`, `harris_tzavalis`, `levin_lin_chu`, `im_pesaran_shin`, `fisher` with Dickey-Fuller and Phillips-Perron, `cips` under a common factor, `hadri`, `kao`, `westerlund`, `pedroni`, `dumitrescu_hurlin`, `pesaran_cd`, `lm_cross_section`, `friedman`, `frees`, `wooldridge`) with close alternatives; a contract test requires a study for every category A test. Findings: `levin_lin_chu` (about 25 %), `harris_tzavalis` (about 9 %) and `fisher` (about 8 %) are oversized at 30 units x 50 periods, and the same design simulated in Stata 17 gives the same rates (the behaviour of `xtunitroot` is reproduced); `friedman` has the degrees-of-freedom rule of `xtcsd` (`N - 1`, correct only when `N = T`), see `docs/size_power.md`.
- Validation gate of the residual and specification tests: size and power by simulation (`validation/simulate.py`, table in `docs/size_power.md`, checked by `tests/integration/test_size_power.py`) and degenerate inputs (`tests/unit/diagnostics/test_edge_cases.py`). Fixes found by them: `as_array` refuses an empty array; `white`, `breusch_pagan`, `wald_groupwise` and `reset` refuse constant squared residuals, a constant dependent variable or no usable unit instead of returning a number or a warning; `skewness_kurtosis` no longer warns on an empty sample; `slope_homogeneity` refuses a single unit and detects a perfect fit by a relative tolerance; `lm_random_effects` adds a remark when the pooled residuals are negatively correlated within units (its one-sided p-value is then not readable without `variance_u`).
- `panel_durbin_watson` (category A, serial correlation; aliases `xtregar`, `lbi`, `bhargava`): modified Bhargava-Franzini-Narendranathan Durbin-Watson statistic and Baltagi-Wu LBI statistic of the within residuals, with holes and any `delta` following the calendar. The result has no p-value (the null distribution depends on the data) and a remark points to the published bounds; no table is embedded because none could be checked against a source. Validated against Stata 17 `xtregar, fe lbi` (`e(d1)`, `e(LBI)`, `e(N_LBI)`; 6 cases, relative difference below 1e-9).
- `slope_homogeneity` (category A, new family `heterogeneity`): Pesaran-Yamagata slope homogeneity test, the standardised form of Swamy's statistic (alias `xthst`; standard and adjusted `Delta` with two-sided p-values and Swamy's `S` with its chi-square p-value in `details`), with `partial` variables, `constant=False`, the `ar` correction and `crosssectional` averages, on balanced and unbalanced panels; also `slope_heterogeneity(panel, ...)` and `PanelData.slope_heterogeneity(...)`. Validated against Stata 17 `xthst` (7 cases, relative difference below 1e-9, with `set type double` because `xthst` builds its cross-sectional averages with `egen`, a float). `xthst` prints p-values as `2 * (1 - normal(|Delta|))`, which lose every digit below 1e-15; the cases compare `2 * normal(-|Delta|)`. The HAC version (`hac`, Blomquist-Westerlund) and `cr_lags` are not taken yet (plan 8.13).
- Normality tests, continued (category B, bare residuals): `shapiro_wilk` (`swilk`, alias `swilk`; Royston's coefficients and normal approximation, exact p-value for n = 3, average ranks for ties or `ties=False`) and `shapiro_francia` (`sfrancia`, alias `sfrancia`; Royston's log transformation or `transform="boxcox"`). Both return W, V and z in `details`. Validated against Stata 17 on every branch of the approximation (n = 3, 4, 5, 7, 11, 12, 15, 900, 1500, 3521, with and without ties); `swilk` at n = 3 with W at its lower bound returns a p-value of 0 where Stata prints a slightly negative one (noted in the result). `swilk`'s `lnnormal` option is not taken.
- Specification tests (category B, bare arrays): `reset` (Ramsey RESET, alias `ovtest`; powers 2-4 of the fitted values or, with `rhs=True`, of each non-binary regressor, rescaled to [0, 1] as Stata does; collinear powers are left out and counted in a remark) and `vif` (variance inflation factors, centered or `uncentered`, a descriptive `VarianceInflation` result like `cross_correlation`, not a test). Validated against Stata 17 `estat ovtest` (to 3e-6, `estat ovtest` builds the powers as single-precision variables; also against the same test computed in double precision in Stata, to 2e-13) and `estat vif` (to 1e-9 with `set type double`, Stata keeps the factors in a float variable). A regressor that is constant or an exact linear combination of the others is refused by `vif` where Stata omits it from the model.
- `breitung` (category A, unit roots): Breitung test of a common AR parameter on forward-orthogonalised data (`deterministics` `none`, `constant`, `trend`; prewhitening `lags`; `demean`; `robust` for the statistic robust to cross-sectional correlation, which needs more periods than units); needs a strongly balanced panel without gaps. Validated against Stata 17 `xtunitroot breitung` (9 cases, relative difference below 1e-9).
- `westerlund` (category A, cointegration): Westerlund (2005) variance-ratio test, `alternative` (`some` or `all` panels), `deterministics` (`constant`, `trend`), `demean`; unbalanced panels allowed (`T` is the longest unit, as in Stata); 1 to 7 regressors.  Validated against `xtcointtest westerlund` of Stata 17 (11 cases, 1e-9).  The standardising means and variances are compiled in Stata's Mata library: `westerlund_moments.csv` was deduced from its output (exact numbers with 8 decimals).
- `pedroni` (category A, cointegration): Pedroni (1999, 2004) panel and group statistics (variance ratio, modified Phillips-Perron t, Phillips-Perron t, ADF t), `ar` (`panelspecific` or `same`), `statistic`, `deterministics` (`constant`, `none`, `trend`), three kernels at a fixed number of lags (`kernel_lags`, required: Stata's automatic bandwidth is not reproduced), ADF lags fixed or chosen by information criteria, `demean`, unbalanced panels; every statistic is in `details`.  Validated against `xtcointtest pedroni` of Stata 17 (20 cases, 1e-9).  `pedroni_moments.csv` (147 adjustment terms, three decimals) was deduced from Stata's output; one of them, the panel modified Phillips-Perron t with no deterministic term and seven regressors, has in Stata the opposite sign of the series and of Pedroni's table: it is reproduced and a note says so.
- `friedman(..., dof="periods")`: the p-value of the distribution of the statistic (`T - 1` degrees of freedom); the default stays that of Stata's `xtcsd` (`N - 1`, right only when `N = T`) and a remark warns when the two differ.  Validated against Stata (`chi2tail(e(g_min) - 1, r(fried))`, 2 cases).
- `cips` (category A, unit roots, alias `pescadf`): Pesaran's cross-sectionally augmented Dickey-Fuller test for panels whose units are correlated through common factors; `lags` (one number or one per unit), `deterministics` (`constant`, `trend`) and `truncate`; returns Z-t-bar with its p-value, and in `options` t-bar and its 1 %, 5 % and 10 % critical values (balanced panels) with the unit statistics in `details`; moments and critical values are Pesaran's tables (`cips_moments.csv`, `cips_critical.csv`) read as `pescadf` does.  Validated against `pescadf` of Stata 17 (13 cases, 1e-9) and `plm::cipstest`.  The removal of the initial cross-section mean of `pescadf` (`nodemean`) is a constant shift absorbed by the regression and has no option.  Z-t-bar assumes independent unit statistics and is oversized under a strong common factor (about 10 %, as `pescadf`): decide with t-bar against its critical values when the panel is balanced.
- `hadri` (category A, unit roots): Hadri LM test whose null is stationarity (right tail), with `deterministics` (`constant`, `trend`), `demean`, the heteroskedasticity-robust statistic and the Bartlett, Parzen and quadratic-spectral long-run variances (`kernel`, `lags`); needs a strongly balanced panel without gaps. Validated against Stata 17 `xtunitroot hadri` (9 cases, relative difference below 1e-9).
- `fisher(..., test="pperron")`: Fisher-type test built on Phillips-Perron p-values (Bartlett Newey-West `lags`, MacKinnon p-value of `Z(t)`, constant or trend), with `Z(rho)` per unit in `details`; brick `diagnostics/_shared/pperron.py`. Validated against Stata 17 `xtunitroot fisher, pperron` (8 cases including units too short to be tested or saturated, entry, exit and holes, relative difference below 1e-9). Finding: as in the Dickey-Fuller case, `pperron` stops with fewer than two equations and returns `p = 1` for a saturated regression, which counts in the degrees of freedom; both are reproduced.
- `kao` (category A, cointegration; new family): Kao residual-based tests of no cointegration with a common cointegrating vector: modified DF t, DF t, ADF t and the two unadjusted DF statistics (`statistic` picks the reported one, all five are in `details`), kernels Bartlett, Parzen and quadratic-spectral with a fixed number of lags or the Newey-West automatic selection of each unit, augmented lags fixed or chosen by AIC, BIC or HQIC, `demean`. `xt.cointegration(panel, y, [x...], test="kao")` and `PanelData.cointegration`. Needs a strongly balanced panel without gaps. Validated against Stata 17 `xtcointtest kao` (26 cases, relative difference below 1e-9, and below 1e-6 for the statistics built on t ratios that Stata passes through a seven-digit text conversion). Findings: Stata's automatic bandwidth is Newey and West (1994) applied to the trace of the autocovariance matrices, with the lags it reports capped but the kernel computed with the uncapped bandwidth; Stata reports `1 - Phi(|z|)` as p-value, this package the left tail.
- `dumitrescu_hurlin` (category A, causality; new family; aliases `granger`, `xtgcause`, `pgrangertest`): Dumitrescu-Hurlin test that `cause` does not Granger-cause `dependent` in any unit of a strongly balanced panel, from the Wald statistic of each unit (`regress y L(1/K).y L(1/K).x`, `test` of the lags of x): `W-bar`, `Z-bar` and `Z-tilde` (exact finite-sample moments, needs T > 3K + 5, default), two-sided p-values, per-unit Walds in `details`. `xt.causality(panel, y, x, test="granger", lags=K)` and `PanelData.causality`. Validated against an independent computation in Stata 17 (`xtgcause` is SSC and was not installed; 5 cases, relative difference below 1e-9) and against R `plm::pgrangertest` (below 1e-11).
- Degenerate inputs (L.7.6): `tests/contract/test_diagnostics_edge_cases.py` runs the 18 tests of category A on 17 degenerate panels (one to five periods, one to three units, constant series, collinear regressor, a unit entirely missing, a hole, an infinite value...) and the category B tests on degenerate arrays.  Every call must return a finite statistic or a refusal with a catalogue key in both languages, never a raw exception, a numerical warning or a NaN statistic; a unit entirely missing is left out as if it were not there (same statistic as the panel without it).  Refusals added where a test returned noise or crashed: `slope_homogeneity` (too few periods for the adjusted statistic), `levin_lin_chu` (a constant unit, named), `harris_tzavalis`, `frees` and `friedman` (a constant series or unit), `wooldridge`, `kao` and `panel_durbin_watson` (a regressor that fits the dependent variable perfectly), `hansen` and `sargan` (zero weight matrix, no group), `hausman` (`keep` out of range).  Where Stata prints a number or a missing value for these inputs (`xtunitroot ht` gives rho = 0 and z = -20 for a constant series), the test refuses instead; the Fisher test keeps Stata's p-value of 1 for a constant series.
- Validation against `pyxtdpthresh` (L.7.4): `validation/cross_pyxtdpthresh.py` freezes, for 11 golden cases of that package, the inputs its estimator hands to its Hansen and Arellano-Bond tests next to the statistics `xtdpthresh` printed in Stata 17 (`tests/golden/overid_ar_pyxtdpthresh.json`); `hansen` and `ar_test` of this package give Stata's J (degrees of freedom and p-value), AR(1), AR(2) and their pieces `b0`, `T1`, `T2 + T3`, joint and conditional, to 1e-8 (`tests/golden/test_against_pyxtdpthresh.py`).
- Refusals checked against Stata (L.7.1): `xtunitroot llc`, `ht`, `breitung`, `hadri` on an unbalanced panel and `xtunitroot ips`, `xtcointtest kao`, `pedroni`, `westerlund` on a panel with holes all stop with `r(498)` in Stata 17; the tests refuse the same panels with the same code (cases `bal_*` and `gap_*`).  The code was 459 and 451, guesses that Stata disproves; it is now 498.  `pescadf` runs on a panel with holes, `cips` refuses it (stated limit).
- `baltagi_li` (category B, effects; aliases `xttest1`, `pbsytest`): Baltagi-Li and Bera-Sosa-Escudero-Yoon Lagrange multiplier tests of random effects and first-order serial correlation from the pooled residuals and the unit boundaries, unbalanced panels allowed: joint LM, marginal and robust LM for each component, and the two one-sided statistics (`statistic=`; all seven in `details`); a panel with gaps is refused with `r(498)` when the periods are given.  Validated against `xttest1` (package `sg164_1` of the Stata Journal 8-1, installed in Stata's PLUS directory for the purpose) and an independent Mata computation on 9 cases (Mata to 1e-9; `xttest1` to 1e-5, it stores the lagged residual as a `float` and returns its one-sided p-values as `1 - normprob(z)`), and against `plm::pbsytest` and `plmtest` of R.  The robust statistics are robust only locally: the size reaches 21 % with a unit effect of sd 1 and 67 % with an autoregressive root of 0.5 (`docs/size_power.md`).
- `slope_homogeneity` HAC version of Blomquist and Westerlund (2013) (`hac=True`, `kernel="bartlett"|"qs"|"truncated"`, `bandwidth=`, `whitening=`; the last two imply `hac`, as in `xthst`) and lags of the cross-sectional averages (`cr_lags=`, one value or one per variable; a row whose lagged average does not exist is dropped).  Validated against `xthst, hac` in Stata 17 on 27 cases to 1e-9, average bandwidth `r(bw)` included, on balanced and unbalanced panels with one, two and three regressors.  The Mata code has several oddities that are reproduced (Bartlett adds `G - G'`, `invsym` reads the upper triangle and drops non-positive pivots, largest pivot first); whitening with three regressors or more can differ from `xthst` because the signs of the singular vectors of Mata's `svd` differ from LAPACK's under SciPy (documented, not validated).  Two size and power studies were added.
